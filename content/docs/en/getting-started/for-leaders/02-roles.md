# Step 2: Setting Up Roles

> This page has been generated by AI and may contain severe factual inaccuracies or mistakes.
> Use at your own risk.

Roles are the foundation of how Horenso SPACE manages access and permissions in your club. A well-designed role structure ensures that members have the right level of access—enough to do their responsibilities, but not so much that they can accidentally (or intentionally) disrupt the club's operations.

## Understanding Roles

A **role** is essentially a collection of permissions packaged together under a meaningful name. Instead of assigning individual permissions to each member, you create roles that represent different types of people in your club, then assign those roles to members.

Think of roles like job titles in a company: "Manager", "Employee", and "Intern" each come with different responsibilities and access levels. Your club works the same way.

### Why Roles Matter

Proper role design provides several benefits:

1. **Security** - Prevents unauthorized access to sensitive features
2. **Clarity** - Members understand their responsibilities
3. **Efficiency** - Assign roles once instead of individual permissions
4. **Scalability** - Easy to add new members with the right access
5. **Accountability** - Clear who can perform which actions

Poor role design, on the other hand, can lead to:

- Members unable to perform their duties
- Too many people with excessive permissions
- Confusion about who's responsible for what
- Accidental deletion or modification of important data

## Permission Types

Before creating roles, you need to understand the available permissions. Each permission controls access to a specific feature or action.

### Report Permissions

These permissions control the absence reporting system:

**Submit absence reports** (`canWriteOwnReport`)  
Allows members to create and submit their own absence reports. This is the most basic member permission—virtually everyone in your club should have this. Without it, members can't notify you about absences.

**View all absence reports** (`canReadAllReports`)  
Grants access to see every member's absence reports, not just your own. This is useful for leaders who need to track attendance, schedule planners, or admins who need oversight. Regular members typically shouldn't have this permission as it involves seeing other people's personal information (absence reasons).

**Review and respond to absence reports** (`canWriteReportReview`)  
Allows adding reviews, comments, and approval marks to absence reports submitted by others. This is a leadership permission—use it for section leaders, team captains, or anyone responsible for approving absences. Note that this permission also implicitly grants viewing all reports.

### Calendar Permissions

These control access to your club's schedule:

**View club calendar events** (`canReadCalendar`)  
Allows members to see the club calendar and all scheduled events. Almost all members should have this permission—it's how they know when to show up! Without it, members are essentially blind to the schedule.

**Create and edit calendar events** (`canWriteCalendar`)  
Grants the ability to add new events to the calendar and modify existing ones. This is a powerful permission that should be limited to leaders, scheduling coordinators, or admins. In the wrong hands, someone could delete important events or create fake ones.

### File Permissions

These manage the shared file system:

**Access club files** (`canReadFiles`)  
Allows downloading and viewing files shared with the club. Most members should have this permission so they can access sheet music, practice schedules, reference documents, or other shared resources.

**Upload and manage club files** (`canWriteFiles`)  
Grants the ability to upload new files, organize the file structure, and delete files. This should be limited to librarians, secretaries, or admins who are responsible for file management. Regular members don't need this level of access.

### Administrative Permission

**Manage club** (`canManageClub`)  
This is the ultimate permission—full administrative access. Members with this permission can:

- Create, edit, and delete roles
- Manage club members (add, remove, change roles)
- Edit club information
- Create and manage invitation links
- Configure absence report forms
- Access all features without restriction

This permission should be highly restricted. Typically, only the club president, vice president, and faculty advisor should have full management access. Too many people with this permission can lead to conflicts and accidental changes.

## Designing Your Role Structure

Now that you understand permissions, you can design roles for your club. Start by thinking about the different types of people in your organization.

### Identifying Role Types

Ask yourself:

- What leadership positions exist in your club?
- What responsibilities does each position have?
- What access does each position need to fulfill those responsibilities?
- Are there any special positions (librarian, equipment manager, etc.)?

Common club structures include:

**Three-Tier Structure** (Most common)

- Admins (president, advisor)
- Leaders (section leaders, captains)
- Members (everyone else)

**Four-Tier Structure** (Larger clubs)

- Admins (president, vice president, advisor)
- Senior Leaders (section leaders, team leads)
- Junior Leaders (assistants, mentors)
- Members (regular participants)

**Specialized Roles** (As needed)

- Admin
- Leader
- Member
- Librarian (file management)
- Secretary (calendar management)
- Equipment Manager (file management for inventories)

Choose a structure that matches your club's actual organization. Don't create roles "just in case"—only make what you'll actually use.

### Example: School Band Structure

Let's design roles for a school marching band:

**Admin Role**  
Who: Band director, assistant directors  
Needs to:

- Manage all aspects of the club
- View and approve all absences
- Manage the full schedule
- Control file access

Permissions:

- ✅ Manage club
- ✅ View all absence reports
- ✅ Review and respond to absence reports
- ✅ Submit absence reports
- ✅ View club calendar events
- ✅ Create and edit calendar events
- ✅ Access club files
- ✅ Upload and manage club files

**Section Leader Role**  
Who: Student leaders for each instrument section  
Needs to:

- Track attendance in their section
- Approve absence requests
- Add section-specific events
- Access teaching resources

Permissions:

- ✅ View all absence reports
- ✅ Review and respond to absence reports
- ✅ Submit absence reports
- ✅ View club calendar events
- ✅ Create and edit calendar events
- ✅ Access club files

**Member Role**  
Who: All band students  
Needs to:

- Submit absence reports
- See the practice and performance schedule
- Access sheet music and announcements

Permissions:

- ✅ Submit absence reports
- ✅ View club calendar events
- ✅ Access club files

**Librarian Role** (Optional specialized role)  
Who: Student in charge of music library  
Needs to:

- Upload and organize sheet music
- Submit their own absences
- See the schedule

Permissions:

- ✅ Submit absence reports

- ✅ Access club files
- ✅ Upload and manage club files

## Creating Roles

Once you've designed your role structure on paper, it's time to create them in Horenso SPACE.

### Step-by-Step Creation

1. **Access Role Management**  
   From your club homepage, click **Config** in the sidebar, then select **Roles**. You'll see your default Admin role listed.

2. **Click Create New Role**  
   This opens the role creation form.

3. **Enter Role Details**

   **Role Name**  
   Choose a clear, descriptive name. Good examples:
   - "Section Leader"
   - "Team Captain"
   - "Club Member"
   - "Equipment Manager"

   Avoid vague names like "Role 2" or "Users". Names should immediately communicate the role's purpose.

   **Description** (Optional but recommended)  
   Explain what this role is for and who should have it. This helps you remember later and provides clarity for members. For example:

   "Section leaders are responsible for their instrument sections, including tracking attendance, running sectionals, and helping new members. This role grants permissions to manage reports and calendar events for their sections."

4. **Select Permissions**  
   Check the boxes for each permission this role should have. Refer back to your designed role structure. Remember: start with minimum necessary permissions. You can always add more later, but taking permissions away can upset members.

5. **Review and Save**  
   Double-check your permissions before clicking **Create**. Make sure the role can accomplish its purpose but doesn't have unnecessary access.

6. **Test the Role**  
   After creating a role, consider creating a test account or using a secondary account to verify the permissions work as expected. This prevents surprises when you assign the role to actual members.

### Creating All Your Roles

Repeat this process for each role in your structure. A typical club will create 3-5 roles:

- 1 Admin role (already exists)
- 1-2 Leadership roles (leaders, senior leaders, etc.)
- 1 Member role (regular participants)
- 0-2 Specialized roles (as needed)

There's no need to rush. You can create additional roles later if you discover you need them.

## Best Practices

Follow these guidelines for effective role management:

**Start Simple**  
Begin with just three roles: Admin, Leader, and Member. You can always add more later as your needs become clearer. Overcomplicating the structure early on creates confusion.

**Grant Minimum Necessary Permissions**  
Each role should have exactly the permissions needed to fulfill its purpose—no more, no less. This principle of "least privilege" reduces security risks and prevents accidents.

**Use Descriptive Names**  
Role names should be self-explanatory. Anyone should be able to guess what "Section Leader" can do, but "Power User" is ambiguous. Good names help everyone understand the club's structure.

**Document Role Purposes**  
Use the description field! Future you (and future admins) will appreciate knowing what each role was designed for. This is especially important if you plan to hand off club management to someone else.

**Review Periodically**  
Every few months, check if your roles still match your club's needs. Organizations evolve, and your permission structure should evolve with them.

**Limit Admin Access**  
Seriously consider who needs full management permissions. Two to three admins is usually ideal—enough for redundancy if someone is unavailable, but not so many that changes become chaotic.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Too Many Roles**  
Don't create a unique role for every single person. If you have ten roles for fifteen people, your structure is too complex. Consolidate roles where possible.

**Everyone Has Admin Access**  
Making all your friends admins might seem nice, but it's a security and management nightmare. Reserve admin access for true administrators.

**No Member Role**  
Don't forget to create a basic member role! This is what most people will have. Without it, you'll need to grant elevated permissions to regular members.

**Inconsistent Permission Logic**  
If leaders in one area have certain permissions, leaders in other areas probably should too. Keep permission levels consistent across similar roles.

**Permission Creep**  
Resist the urge to add permissions to roles just because someone asks. Evaluate whether the permission is truly necessary for that role's purpose.
