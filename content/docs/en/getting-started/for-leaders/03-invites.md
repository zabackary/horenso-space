# Step 3: Inviting Members

> This page has been generated by AI and may contain severe factual inaccuracies or mistakes.
> Use at your own risk.

Now that you've created your club and set up a role structure, it's time to invite your members to join. Horenso SPACE uses invitation links to manage member onboarding—a secure, flexible system that lets you control who joins your club and with what permissions.

## Understanding Invitation Links

Invitation links are special URLs that allow people to join your club with a pre-assigned role. When someone clicks an invitation link, they're taken to a page showing your club's name and the role they'll receive. After they create an account (or log in to an existing one), they're automatically added to your club with the specified permissions.

This system offers several advantages over traditional invitation methods:

**Security Through Expiration**  
Each invitation link can have an expiration date. After that date, the link stops working, preventing unauthorized access if the link is shared beyond your intended audience. This is particularly useful when sharing links in semi-public spaces like social media or club websites.

**Role Pre-Assignment**  
You can create different invitation links for different roles. Send one link to prospective section leaders and a different link to regular members. This eliminates the need to manually adjust permissions after people join.

**Unlimited Use**  
A single invitation link can be used by multiple people until it expires or is manually deleted. This makes it easy to share one link in a group chat or email, rather than generating individual invitations for each member.

**Revocable Access**  
If an invitation link is compromised or you change your mind about recruiting, simply delete the link. Anyone who hasn't already used it won't be able to join anymore.

## Creating Your First Invitation

Let's walk through creating an invitation link for regular club members:

### Step 1: Access Invitation Management

From your club homepage, navigate to **Config** in the sidebar, then select **Invites**. You'll see a page showing any existing invitation links (which will be empty if this is your first time here).

### Step 2: Click Create New Invitation

This opens a form where you'll configure the invitation parameters.

### Step 3: Select the Role

Choose which role invited members will receive when they join. For your first invitation, select the "Member" role you created in the previous step. This ensures new members start with appropriate, limited permissions rather than elevated access.

Think carefully about which role you're assigning. You'll create separate invitations for different roles, so don't worry about inviting everyone with this one link.

### Step 4: Set an Expiration Date

Choose when this invitation link should stop working. The system will suggest a date 30 days in the future, but you can adjust this based on your needs:

**Short Expirations** (1-7 days)  
Use short expirations when:

- Recruiting at a specific event (club fair, open house)
- Running a limited-time recruitment drive
- Sharing the link in a public space
- You want tight control over membership

Short expirations are more secure but require you to create new invitations more frequently.

**Medium Expirations** (7-30 days)  
Use medium expirations when:

- Starting a new season or semester
- Initial club setup (like right now!)
- Sharing in semi-private spaces (email, private group chats)
- Recruiting throughout a defined period

This is the most common choice. It's long enough to catch most interested members but short enough to maintain security.

**Long Expirations** (30-90 days)  
Use long expirations when:

- Your club has rolling admission throughout the year
- You're embedding the link on a website
- Membership is open-ended
- You're comfortable with longer-term access

Long expirations are convenient but require more trust in where the link is shared.

**No Expiration**  
Generally not recommended. Even if your club has permanent open enrollment, we suggest using 90-day links and renewing them as needed. This gives you regular opportunities to review and refresh your membership approach.

### Step 5: Create and Copy the Link

After clicking **Create**, the system generates a unique invitation URL. It will look something like:

```
https://horenso-space.app/app/club/invite/abc123xyz789
```

Copy this link immediately. You'll use it to share with prospective members. The link is also permanently accessible from the Invites page, so don't worry if you lose it—you can always come back and copy it again.

## Distributing Invitations

Now that you have an invitation link, you need to get it to your prospective members. The distribution method depends on your club's communication channels and membership context.

### Email

Email is the most professional distribution method, especially for school clubs or formal organizations. Compose a welcoming message that includes:

**Subject Line Examples:**

- "Join [Club Name] on Horenso SPACE"
- "Welcome to [Club Name] - Get Started Here"
- "[Club Name] Membership - Action Required"

**Body Template:**

```
Hi [First Name / Team / Everyone],

We're excited to welcome you to [Club Name]! We use Horenso SPACE to manage our schedule, track attendance, and share important information.

To join our club:
1. Click this link: [invitation link]
2. Create your account (or log in if you already have one)
3. You'll automatically be added to our club

After joining, you'll be able to:
- View our practice and performance schedule
- Submit absence reports when you can't attend
- Access shared files and resources

If you have any questions, feel free to reply to this email or ask at our next meeting.

Looking forward to working with you!

[Your Name]
[Your Title]
[Club Name]
```

### Group Chat (LINE, Discord, Slack, WhatsApp)

Group chats are informal and immediate. Use this method when your members are already in an existing chat group:

**Message Template:**

```
Hey everyone! 👋

We're now using Horenso SPACE for club management.

Join here: [invitation link]

Once you join, you'll be able to see our schedule and submit absence reports through the app. Let me know if you have any issues!
```

Keep the message brief for chat apps—people are less likely to read long messages in group chats.

### Social Media

If your club has a social media presence and recruiting is open, you can post invitation links publicly. However, take extra precautions:

- Use short expiration dates (3-7 days)
- Only share Member role invitations, never Leader or Admin
- Monitor who joins and remove unauthorized members promptly
- Consider requiring approval at an in-person meeting

**Social Post Template:**

```
🎉 Interested in joining [Club Name]?

We're recruiting new members! Sign up through our Horenso SPACE link: [invitation link]

(Link expires [date])

DM us with any questions!
```

### QR Codes

For in-person recruitment events (club fairs, open houses, performances), generate a QR code from your invitation link. Members can scan it with their phones to join on the spot.

To create a QR code:

1. Copy your invitation link
2. Use a free QR code generator (search "QR code generator")
3. Paste your link and download the QR code image
4. Print the QR code on flyers, posters, or table displays

Include text near the QR code: "Scan to join [Club Name] on Horenso SPACE"

### Club Website

If your club maintains a website, you can embed the invitation link on a "Join Us" page. Since websites are semi-public, follow these practices:

- Use medium-length expirations (30 days) and update regularly
- Place the link behind a login or membership form if possible
- Include clear expectations: "Current members should use this link to access our scheduling system"
- Monitor your member list for unexpected additions

## Creating Multiple Invitations

Most clubs need several different invitation links for different purposes and roles. Here's a recommended approach:

### Invitation for Regular Members

This is your primary invitation for most people joining the club.

- **Role:** Member
- **Expiration:** 30 days
- **Distribution:** Email, group chat, club fair

### Invitation for Leaders

Create a separate link for people who should have leadership permissions.

- **Role:** Section Leader (or your equivalent)
- **Expiration:** 7-14 days (tighter control for elevated permissions)
- **Distribution:** Direct email to specific individuals

**Important:** Only share leadership invitations with people you've explicitly chosen for leadership roles. Don't post these in group chats or public spaces.

### Invitation for Co-Admins

If you need additional administrators, create an admin-role invitation.

- **Role:** Admin
- **Expiration:** 3-7 days (very short for maximum security)
- **Distribution:** Direct, private communication only

Treat admin invitations like passwords—share them only with fully trusted individuals through secure channels.

### Rolling Recruitment Invitation

If your club accepts members year-round, maintain an ongoing invitation link.

- **Role:** Member
- **Expiration:** 90 days
- **Distribution:** Club website, permanent bulletin board posting
- **Maintenance:** Set a calendar reminder to renew every 80 days

## Managing Existing Invitations

Your Invites page shows all active invitation links with their details: role, expiration date, and number of uses. Regular maintenance keeps your membership secure and organized.

### Monitoring Usage

Check your invitations page weekly, especially after sending out new links. Look for:

- **Unexpectedly high usage** - Might indicate the link was shared beyond your intended audience
- **Zero usage after several days** - Might mean members aren't seeing or understanding your invitation
- **Usage right before expiration** - Members procrastinating; consider sending reminders

### Deleting Expired or Unwanted Invitations

Clean up your invitations list by deleting:

- Links that have expired
- Links that have served their purpose (everyone invited has joined)
- Links that were accidentally created with wrong settings
- Compromised links that were shared inappropriately

To delete an invitation:

1. Go to **Config** → **Invites**
2. Find the invitation in the list
3. Click the delete or remove button
4. Confirm deletion

Deleted invitations stop working immediately. Anyone who hasn't joined yet won't be able to use that link anymore.

### Renewing Invitations

When an important invitation is about to expire but you still need it, create a new one with identical settings rather than trying to modify the existing one. This gives you a fresh expiration period and a new link to share.

Before deleting the old link, announce the new one to your members: "Heads up: if you haven't joined yet, use this new link instead: [new link]. The old link expires tomorrow."

## Security Best Practices

Invitation links are powerful tools, but they require responsible management:

**Never Share Admin Invitations Publicly**  
Admin-role invitations should only go to specific, trusted individuals through private communication channels. Treat them like passwords.

**Use Appropriate Expirations**  
The more public your distribution method, the shorter your expiration should be. Public social media posts should have 3-7 day expirations. Private emails can have 30-60 day expirations.

**Review Your Member List Regularly**  
After sending out invitations, check **Config** → **Members** to see who has joined. Remove anyone who shouldn't have access. Early detection prevents problems.

**Create Separate Links for Different Roles**  
Never use a single invitation link for both members and leaders. This prevents accidental permission elevation and makes it easier to track who should have which role.

**Update Links on Public Channels**  
If you've posted an invitation link on your club website or social media, set a calendar reminder to replace it with a new link before it expires. This prevents broken links and maintains security.

**Don't Reuse Compromised Links**  
If an invitation link has been shared inappropriately (posted where you didn't intend, shared outside your club, etc.), delete it immediately and create a fresh one. Don't take chances with security.

## Troubleshooting Common Issues

**Members Say the Link Doesn't Work**  
First, check if the invitation has expired. If yes, create a new one and share it. If no, verify the member is copying the entire URL, including the https:// part. Some email clients or messaging apps might truncate links.

**Too Many People Joined**  
If your invitation link was shared more broadly than intended, review your member list and remove unauthorized members. Delete the compromised invitation and create a new one with better distribution control.

**Nobody Is Joining**  
Members might not have seen your invitation, might not understand what it's for, or might be procrastinating. Send a reminder message with clearer instructions. Consider offering to help people join at your next meeting.

**Members Joined with the Wrong Role**  
This happens when people use the wrong invitation link. You can fix this after the fact: go to **Config** → **Members**, find the person, and change their role to the correct one.
