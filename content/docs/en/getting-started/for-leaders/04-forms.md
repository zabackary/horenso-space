# Step 4: Configuring Absence Forms

> This page has been generated by AI and may contain severe factual inaccuracies or mistakes.
> Use at your own risk.

Absence reports are how your members communicate when they can't attend club activities. The form they fill out determines what information you collect and how useful those reports are for tracking attendance and understanding absence patterns. A well-designed form makes reporting easy for members while giving leaders the data they need to make informed decisions.

## Why Custom Forms Matter

Horenso SPACE provides a default absence report form, but it's intentionally basic. Different clubs have different needs—what a robotics team needs to know might be completely different from what a theater group or sports team requires. Customizing your form ensures you collect exactly the information that matters to your organization, no more and no less.

**Benefits of Thoughtful Form Design:**

- **Relevant Information** - Collect data specific to your club's needs
- **Better Decision Making** - Having the right information helps leaders approve or deny requests appropriately
- **Clear Communication** - Well-structured forms reduce confusion about what members should report
- **Efficiency** - Properly designed forms reduce back-and-forth questions
- **Consistency** - Everyone provides the same information, making it easier to compare and track patterns

## Understanding Form Fields

Absence report forms are built using different types of fields, each collecting a specific type of information. Understanding these field types helps you design effective forms.

### Text Fields

Text fields allow members to type free-form explanations. They're the most flexible field type because members can write whatever they need, but they're also the least structured—you might get very detailed explanations or just a word or two.

**When to use text fields:**

- Asking for absence reasons
- Requesting additional details or context
- Allowing members to explain circumstances
- Any situation where you need open-ended responses

**Example prompts:**

- "Please explain why you'll be absent"
- "Additional information (optional)"
- "How can we help you catch up on what you'll miss?"

**Best practices:**

- Make them required if you need an explanation
- Provide clear examples of good responses
- Keep prompts specific to avoid vague answers
- Use optional text fields for supplementary information

### Date and Time Fields

These fields collect temporal information about the absence. While the system knows which calendar event the report is for, you might need additional date/time information for partial absences or specific circumstances.

**When to use date/time fields:**

- Partial attendance ("I'll arrive late" or "I need to leave early")
- Multi-day absences spanning several events
- Specific timing details ("I need to leave by 5 PM for another commitment")

**Example prompts:**

- "What time do you need to leave?"
- "When will you arrive?"
- "Last date you'll be able to attend before the absence"

**Best practices:**

- Only ask if timing is truly important to track
- Make them optional if most people won't need them
- Clarify whether you want just date, just time, or both

### Dropdown Menus (Select Fields)

Dropdown menus present members with a list of predefined options to choose from. This is one of the most useful field types because it standardizes responses, making it easy to analyze patterns and categorize absences.

**When to use dropdowns:**

- Categorizing absence reasons
- Selecting priority levels
- Choosing from limited options
- Any situation where you want consistent, analyzable data

**Example prompts and options:**

"Reason for absence:"

- Illness
- Family commitment
- Academic conflict
- Work obligation
- Personal emergency
- Other

"Have you arranged coverage for your responsibilities?"

- Yes, [name] will cover for me
- No, I need help arranging coverage
- Not applicable

**Best practices:**

- Include an "Other" option for situations you haven't anticipated
- Keep option lists manageable (5-10 options max)
- Use clear, mutually exclusive categories
- Order options logically (most common first, or alphabetically)

### Checkbox Fields

Checkboxes are for yes/no questions or acknowledgments. Members simply check or leave unchecked a box to indicate their response.

**When to use checkboxes:**

- Confirming the member has read and understood policies
- Yes/no questions with clear answers
- Acknowledgments ("I understand this may affect my participation")
- Multiple-choice selections where more than one answer is allowed

**Example prompts:**

- "☐ I understand that excessive absences may affect my standing in the club"
- "☐ I have reviewed the attendance policy"
- "☐ This is an emergency absence (submitted less than 24 hours before the event)"

**Best practices:**

- Use clear, unambiguous language
- Make required if acknowledgment is mandatory
- Consider the default state (checked vs unchecked)
- Don't overuse—too many checkboxes feel bureaucratic

## Designing Your Form

Before building your form in the system, sketch it out on paper or in a document. Think through what information you truly need and how members will interact with the form.

### Start with Your Requirements

Ask yourself and your leadership team:

- What do we need to know about every absence?
- What information helps us make approval decisions?
- What details help us support the absent member?
- What data would help us understand attendance patterns?

**Example for a School Band:**

_Core needs:_

- Which rehearsal or performance they're missing
- Basic reason (illness, family, academic, etc.)
- Expected return date
- Whether they need makeup work or sectional time

_Nice to have:_

- Specific illness details
- Whether they've notified their section leader
- If they need music or assignments emailed

_Don't need:_

- Extremely personal medical details
- Hour-by-hour schedule of their day
- Contact information (you already have this)

### Design for Your Members

Remember that members will be filling out this form, often on mobile devices, sometimes in a rush. Make it as easy as possible while still collecting necessary information.

**Principles of member-friendly forms:**

**Brevity** - Ask only what you need. Every additional field increases the chance members will abandon the form or provide low-quality answers.

**Clarity** - Use simple, direct language. Avoid jargon or ambiguous terms. If a field might be confusing, add a brief explanation.

**Logical Flow** - Order fields in a natural sequence. Start with the basics (which event, general reason) before asking for details.

**Appropriate Field Types** - Use dropdowns for categorization, text fields for explanations, and checkboxes for acknowledgments. Don't use a text field when a dropdown would work better.

**Mobile Friendly** - Most members will submit reports from their phones. Keep this in mind—don't create forms with so many fields that they require extensive scrolling.

### Sample Form Designs

Here are examples for different club types:

**Competitive Sports Team:**

```
Required Fields:
- Event: [Auto-selected from calendar]
- Reason: [Dropdown: Injury, Illness, Academic, Family, Other]
- Expected Return: [Date field]
- Details: [Text field] "Please provide additional context"

Optional Fields:
- Have you consulted with the coach? [Checkbox]
- Do you need modified practice plans? [Checkbox]
```

**Theater Production:**

```
Required Fields:
- Rehearsal/Performance: [Auto-selected from calendar]
- Reason: [Dropdown: Illness, Conflict, Emergency, Other]
- Explanation: [Text field]
- Will this affect any performances? [Checkbox if it's during tech week/show week]

Optional Fields:
- Have you arranged an understudy? [Dropdown: Yes - name, No - need help, Not applicable]
- Notes for director: [Text field]
```

**School Club (General):**

```
Required Fields:
- Event: [Auto-selected from calendar]
- Reason: [Dropdown: Illness, Academic, Family, Work, Other]
- Details: [Text field]

Optional Fields:
- Expected return date: [Date field]
- ☐ I understand this counts toward my absence total
```

**Volunteer Organization:**

```

- Event: [Auto-selected from calendar]
- Reason: [Dropdown: Personal, Work conflict, Health, Other commitment, Other]

- Alternate dates you're available: [Text field]
- Additional notes: [Text field]
```

## Creating Your Form

Now let's build your form in Horenso SPACE.

### Accessing Form Management

From your club homepage, go to **Config** → **Absence Forms**. You'll see the default form that's currently active. You can either modify this form or create a new one from scratch.

### Creating a New Form

1. Click **Create New Form**
2. Give your form a descriptive name (this is internal—members won't see it)
   - Examples: "Standard Absence Report", "Performance Absence Form", "Practice Absence Form"

3. Begin adding fields one at a time

### Adding Fields

For each field you designed:

1. Click **Add Field**
2. Select the field type (Text, Date/Time, Dropdown, Checkbox)
3. Enter the field label—this is what members see
4. Configure field-specific options:
   - For dropdowns: add each option on a separate line
   - For text fields: set whether it's single-line or multi-line
   - For all fields: mark as required or optional
5. Add any help text or examples if needed
6. Save the field

Repeat until you've added all your designed fields.

### Ordering Fields

The order in which you add fields is the order members will see them. If you need to rearrange:

- Most systems provide drag-and-drop reordering
- Or you can delete and re-add fields in the correct order
- Consider the natural flow: general → specific, required → optional

### Assigning to Roles

After creating your form, assign it to specific roles. This lets you have different forms for different types of members. For example:

- Regular members use a simple form
- Leaders use a more detailed form that includes delegation information
- Admins might not need a form at all since they're handling reports, not submitting them

To assign:

1. In the form settings, look for "Assign to Roles"
2. Select which roles should use this form
3. Save

Members with multiple roles will use the form with the most permissions, so design accordingly.

### Testing Your Form

Before announcing it to all members, test the form yourself:

1. Create a test absence report using the form
2. Fill it out as a member would
3. Check that:
   - All fields display correctly
   - Required fields are actually required
   - Dropdown options make sense
   - The form isn't overwhelming or confusing
   - Instructions are clear

4. Submit and review the report as a leader would receive it
5. Make adjustments based on what you discover

It's much easier to fix issues now than after dozens of members have submitted reports.

## Maintaining Your Forms

Forms aren't set-it-and-forget-it. You'll need to update them as your club's needs evolve.

### When to Update

Consider updating your form when:

- Members frequently ask the same question you didn't include
- You realize you're collecting information you never use
- Club policies change
- You notice patterns in incomplete or unclear responses
- Seasonal needs change (regular season vs playoffs, rehearsals vs performances)

### How to Update

You have two options:

**Edit the Existing Form** (for minor changes)

- Wording improvements
- Adding optional fields
- Adjusting dropdown options
- Clarifying instructions

**Create a New Form** (for major changes)

- Complete redesign
- Different field structure
- Separate seasonal forms
- Role-specific forms

Creating new forms preserves historical data in the old format while moving forward with new requirements.

### Communicating Changes

When you update the form, tell your members:

- What changed and why
- When the new form takes effect
- Any new expectations or requirements

A quick announcement prevents confusion and frustrated members wondering why the form looks different.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Asking for Too Much** - Every additional field reduces completion rates. If you don't truly need it, don't ask for it.

**Vague Dropdown Options** - Options like "Personal reason" or "Other reason" aren't helpful. Be specific enough to be meaningful but broad enough to cover situations.

**Making Everything Required** - Required fields should be limited to truly necessary information. Making everything required frustrates members and doesn't improve data quality.

**No "Other" Category** - Always include an "Other" option in dropdowns. You can't anticipate every possible situation.

**Confusing Language** - Use plain, simple language. Avoid club-specific jargon that new members might not understand.

**Ignoring Mobile Users** - Most members will submit reports from phones. Test your form on a phone before deploying it widely.
