# Step 6: Establishing Management Routines

> This page has been generated by AI and may contain severe factual inaccuracies or mistakes.
> Use at your own risk.

You've built the foundation: your club exists, roles are defined, members are invited, forms are configured, and the calendar is populated. Now comes the ongoing work—the daily, weekly, and monthly tasks that keep your club functioning smoothly.

Effective club management isn't about working harder; it's about working smarter with consistent routines. When you establish regular habits for common tasks, management becomes less stressful and more sustainable. You'll catch small issues before they become big problems, stay on top of attendance, and maintain clear communication with your members.

This guide will help you build management routines that work for your schedule and your club's needs.

## The Core Management Loop

At its heart, club management follows a repeating cycle:

1. **Plan** - Look ahead at upcoming events, anticipate needs
2. **Communicate** - Tell members what's happening and what you need from them
3. **Monitor** - Check absence reports, track attendance patterns, respond to issues
4. **Adjust** - Update plans, schedules, and approaches based on what you're learning

This cycle happens at multiple time scales simultaneously: daily, weekly, monthly, and seasonally. Let's break down what each time scale requires.

## Daily Management Tasks

These are quick check-ins that keep you informed and responsive. Most leaders spend 5-10 minutes per day on these tasks.

### Check Absence Reports

Look at newly submitted reports for upcoming events. Your main goals:

- Confirm you received the report (many systems auto-notify, but checking manually shows you care)
- Assess whether the absence affects your planning for that event
- Identify patterns (is the same person missing repeatedly?)
- Respond if the report needs clarification or if you have questions

For reports that need review (feedback to members), prioritize those from events happening soon. Members appreciate timely responses, especially if they're unsure whether their reason was acceptable.

### Scan the Calendar

Take 60 seconds to glance at the next 3-5 days of events. Ask yourself:

- Are all necessary events posted?
- Have any events changed that I forgot to update?
- Do I need to prepare anything special for upcoming events?
- Should I remind members about anything unusual coming up?

This quick habit prevents surprises and keeps you mentally prepared.

### Monitor Communication Channels

If your club uses group chats, email, or other communication tools outside Horenso SPACE, check for:

- Questions about events or schedules
- Reports of problems or conflicts
- Informal absence notifications ("Hey, I can't make it tomorrow")
- Member concerns that need addressing

Even if someone reports an absence verbally or through chat, encourage them to also submit it through the formal system so you have a record.

## Weekly Management Tasks

Set aside 15-30 minutes once per week for these more substantial tasks. Many leaders pick a consistent day and time (Sunday evening, Monday morning) to build the habit.

### Review Attendance Patterns

Look at absence reports across the past week and the upcoming week:

- Who's missing frequently? Do they need a check-in conversation?
- Are absences clustered around certain events? (Might indicate scheduling issues)
- Are absence reasons consistent or changing?
- Do you have enough attendance for upcoming events to proceed as planned?

Patterns tell stories. A member who suddenly starts missing regularly might be struggling with workload, losing interest, or facing personal issues. Early conversations can help before a minor issue becomes a dropout.

### Update the Next Two Weeks of Calendar

Add events for the second week out, and verify the first week's events are accurate:

- Confirm times and locations haven't changed
- Add any special instructions to event descriptions
- Remove or mark as cancelled any events that won't happen
- Add new events as soon as you know about them

Staying two weeks ahead gives members enough notice to plan while keeping your workload manageable.

### Process Pending Role or Membership Changes

If you have pending invitations, follow up:

- Resend invites that might have been missed
- Reach out personally to people who haven't accepted
- Remove expired or declined invitations to keep your list clean

If members have asked about role changes or additional permissions, process those requests. Clear communication about why someone got (or didn't get) a role prevents resentment.

### Send a Weekly Update (Optional but Valuable)

Consider sending a brief weekly message to all members with:

- Upcoming events for the next week
- Any schedule changes or special instructions
- Reminders about deadlines or requirements
- Shout-outs to members who contributed something noteworthy

This doesn't need to be long—a few bullet points keep everyone aligned and informed. Many leaders use a standard template to make this faster.

## Monthly Management Tasks

Once per month, step back for bigger-picture review. Set aside 30-60 minutes for these tasks.

### Analyze Attendance Trends

Look at the full month of absence data:

- Calculate attendance rates (how many people at average event?)
- Identify your most and least attended events
- Spot members with concerning absence patterns
- Compare current month to previous months

This analysis informs future planning. If Saturday events consistently get low turnout, maybe Saturday isn't a good day for your club. If certain members are absent more than present, you may need to have difficult conversations about expectations and commitment.

### Review and Adjust Roles

Are your current roles working as designed?

- Are any roles too broad or too narrow in permissions?
- Do you need to create new roles for new situations?
- Should anyone be promoted or given additional access?
- Are there inactive members whose roles should be adjusted?

Roles should evolve as your club evolves. Don't let outdated role structures hinder your operations.

### Update Absence Report Forms

If needed, adjust your absence report form:

- Add or remove fields based on what information you actually use
- Clarify questions that members seem to misunderstand
- Update dropdown options to match current reality

Forms should match your current needs, not what you thought you'd need months ago.

### Evaluate Calendar Effectiveness

Look at your scheduling over the past month:

- Were events scheduled with enough advance notice?
- Did schedule changes cause confusion?
- Are event descriptions giving members the information they need?
- Do event timings reflect reality (practices running long, etc.)?

Use this evaluation to improve your calendar practices going forward.

### Hold a Leadership Check-In

If you have co-leaders or officers, meet monthly to discuss:

- What's going well
- What's challenging
- Changes to consider
- Division of management responsibilities

Shared leadership is more sustainable than one person doing everything.

## Seasonal or Phase-Based Tasks

Certain management tasks align with the club's season or academic calendar rather than fixed time intervals.

### Start of Season/Semester

- Review all roles and update as needed for the new period
- Update absence form fields if your club's focus is shifting
- Populate the calendar with known events for the entire season
- Send a "welcome back" or "season start" message outlining expectations
- Verify all returning members still have appropriate access
- Onboard new leaders or officers who are taking on responsibilities

### Mid-Season Check-In

- Survey members about how things are going (informal or formal survey)
- Assess whether initial plans are working or need adjustment
- Recognize and celebrate milestones or achievements
- Address any ongoing issues that have festered

### End of Season/Semester

- Thank members for their participation
- Collect feedback about what worked and what didn't
- Document lessons learned for next season's leaders
- Archive or clean up old calendar events
- Update roles for the off-season (some members might not need access when inactive)
- Plan leadership transition if applicable

## Delegation and Shared Management

You don't have to do everything yourself. Effective leaders delegate tasks to members with appropriate roles.

### What to Delegate

Consider sharing these responsibilities:

- **Calendar management** - An officer adds events and updates the schedule
- **Absence review** - Multiple leaders review different subsets of reports
- **Communication** - Social media, newsletters, or weekly updates handled by a designated member
- **Attendance tracking** - Someone analyzes patterns and reports to leadership
- **Event coordination** - Different members take point on specific events

### How to Delegate Effectively

1. **Give clear responsibilities** - "You're in charge of updating the calendar every Monday" is clearer than "help with the calendar"
2. **Grant appropriate permissions** - Use roles to give delegated members the access they need
3. **Set expectations** - Explain what success looks like and when tasks should be done
4. **Check in regularly** - Especially early on, verify delegated tasks are happening
5. **Give feedback** - Thank people for good work and gently correct issues

Delegation builds leadership capacity in other members and prevents burnout in yourself.

## Handling Common Management Challenges

### Challenge: Too Many Absence Reports to Review

**Solutions:**

- Only require detailed review for absences meeting certain criteria (frequent absences, important events)
- Delegate report review to multiple leaders
- Adjust your form to auto-categorize reports, letting you focus on those needing attention
- Trust members more—not every report needs deep scrutiny

### Challenge: Members Not Submitting Reports

**Solutions:**

- Send reminders before events
- Make the form easier and faster to complete
- Explain why reports matter (planning, fairness, record-keeping)
- Recognize and thank members who consistently report absences properly
- Have conversations with repeat non-reporters

### Challenge: Calendar Gets Outdated

**Solutions:**

- Set a recurring reminder for calendar updates
- Delegate calendar management to someone more organized
- Keep a running list of calendar changes so you can batch-update
- Use a calendar planning tool outside Horenso SPACE, then transfer weekly

### Challenge: Leadership Burnout

**Solutions:**

- Delegate more tasks to capable members
- Set boundaries on your availability (e.g., "I check reports once per day, not constantly")
- Share leadership with co-leaders
- Simplify processes that take too much time
- Take breaks during off-season periods

Remember: an imperfect but sustainable management approach is better than a perfect approach you can't maintain.

## Building Your Personal Routine

Everyone's life is different, so your routine should fit your schedule. Here's how to build a sustainable personal system:

1. **Identify Your Available Time Slots** - When do you reliably have 5-10 minutes? When can you carve out 30 minutes weekly?

2. **Match Tasks to Time Slots** - Put quick daily tasks in small time windows (checking absence reports during lunch). Reserve longer weekly tasks for when you have focused time.

3. **Use Reminders** - Set recurring phone or calendar reminders for management tasks until they become habit.

4. **Create Checklists** - Write down your daily/weekly/monthly checklists so you don't have to remember everything.

5. **Batch Similar Tasks** - Do all your calendar updates at once, all your communication at once, all your reviews at once. Context-switching wastes time.

6. **Track What Works** - After a month, reflect on which tasks felt easy and which felt burdensome. Adjust your routine accordingly.

The goal is to make management feel effortless through habit rather than relying on motivation and memory.

## Continuous Improvement

Your management approach should evolve as you learn what works for your club. Every semester or season, ask yourself:

- Which tasks took more time than expected?
- Which tasks turned out to be unnecessary?
- What problems kept recurring?
- What did other leaders do that worked well?
- What feedback did members give about club operations?

Then adjust your processes, forms, calendar practices, and routines based on what you learned. The best club leaders are always experimenting and refining.
